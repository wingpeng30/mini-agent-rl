# v123 candidate quality and audit-failure analysis

This is a read-only follow-up on the one frozen v123 pool. No question was added or replaced, no vote was changed, and no new reviewer or model call was made. The recomputable aggregates are in `data/evidence-sft-v123/diagnosis/v123-candidate-failures.json`; per-question details remain private in the adjacent JSONL. The structural replay is in `v123-structural-loss-report.json`. Both reports bind the frozen inputs and votes by SHA-256. All case examples below refer to `internal/review/internal-packets.jsonl` and the saved API/Luna vote files, not to final-test or the sealed independent 100.

## Where the 100 new questions were lost

The first screen used each source question alone with its own BM25 index. It found 45 provisional scope-hint questions among the first 5,000 unexposed source questions, then selected those 45 plus 55 others. The hint was a wording filter, not a semantic scope label. After adding all selected contexts to the one final 2,979-document corpus, only 78/100 new questions retained a search/answer pair. Of the 22 structural losses, eight had no nonempty search state under the shared corpus, thirteen had search and answer examples but no compatible prefix trajectory, and one exceeded the packet token limit. These are largely corpus-and-construction interactions; they do not establish that all 22 source questions are intrinsically bad. The final shared corpus and tokenized pair must be preflighted before any future one-time freeze.

Among the 78 pairable new questions, the saved API tickets approved both states for 25, rejected at least one state for 22, and left at least one state invalid for 31. The 45 old v122 approved questions had only 28 API-approved pairs after the new corpus and exact-input reuse checks. By action, across all 246 states, API approved 96 answers and 60 searches, rejected 21 answers and 24 searches, and produced invalid records for 6 answers and 39 searches. The search side is the more fragile link. Of 19 valid API-rejected new searches, 18 were marked `query_grounded=no`, eight `retrieval_relevant=no`, and three `search_needed=no`; these labels overlap and are reviewer judgments, not verified root causes. A search can retrieve a useful document while its query targets the wrong relation.

The scope proxy failed in this pool. Thirty-six of the 45 new scope-hint questions remained pairable, but only seven had an API-approved pair. None of the new API-approved states carried a `scope` label. All 15 new states given `scope` by valid API tickets were rejected (eight answers, seven searches). Some source questions really are ambiguous or incomplete: case `906df5039cd6f00583b3` asks for an album **and** KFC headquarters, yet its official proposed answer is only an album; case `e99b033bb20fb866c7fb` asks for a country but proposes “South Indian cinema.” Case `2385e9adfc00dd97b83e` searches only for Ames while the still-missing side is Zayre. Such cases should not be repaired by relabeling them as scope successes. The provisional wording filter also cannot guarantee a usable comparison boundary.

## Audit and teacher-construction failures

The 45 invalid API records are a transport/validation category, not 45 semantic rejections: 37 ended with a non-boolean `approved`, six from the first batch had the original combined case/boolean error, and two cited text not visible in the specified document. Thirty-nine of the 45 invalids were searches. The transport kept error classes and request hashes but not the raw invalid response body, so whether those non-boolean replies meant approval, denial, or uncertainty is unknown. The one allowed technical correction after the first batch did not eliminate this failure. Do not coerce these votes or retry them to rescue v123; a future protocol needs raw-response diagnostics in restricted storage and a tested schema before another pool is frozen.

The synthetic search builder generates candidates from visible document titles and full sentences, then selects a query using *official annotated supporting facts* as an oracle for whether retrieval closes a gap. The query text itself is drawn from pre-action material, but the selection objective is answer-aware and can reward a semantically poor query that happens to retrieve the target document. In the new states, 62/78 search queries were a visible text span or a question plus visible seed. Case `c8d256260a9adbe58f68` queried “Caineville, Utah” for Utah's eastern border; the returned Utah page happened to contain Colorado. Luna rejected the query as off-target while DeepSeek approved it. Case `e529d32f46a2002c9250` searched again when the city Budapest was already visible. These demonstrate why retrieval success alone cannot certify search necessity or query quality. They do not measure model-generated search behavior.

The auditors also made observable semantic errors. DeepSeek rejected answer case `a6cd61cef547e6293793` partly because no **single** document states that Joan Horvath worked sixteen years in California, although one visible document identifies her 16-year workplace as JPL and another places JPL in California. It similarly rejected `1fa491912b01a051fd8c` for joining the film's director Woody Allen to the visible Woody Allen biography. Multi-document joins are the point of these questions; the rubric prohibits fabricating a quotation across documents, not reasoning across them. These two cases still have other non-approved paired states, so correcting only those answer judgments would not by itself make a complete pair. In the opposite direction, DeepSeek approved redundant searches for the already-visible football and Budapest answers (`c1656960c8b6359c439a`, `e529d32f46a2002c9250`); Luna rejected them. Luna's rejection of answer `3a13592e3b1e37bf9a1b` says the prior Utah document did not identify its eastern neighbor, but the actual prior Utah text explicitly says Colorado is to the east. That is a definite reviewer reading error. These examples are diagnostic findings, not replacement independent votes.

The mechanical validator also accepted two reused, approved searches whose `missing_fact` field was literally `none`, because it only checked that the field was nonempty. This weakens the meaning of “valid approved search.” A versioned future audit contract should reject nonspecific placeholders, enforce per-document exact quotations and action timing, permit supported multi-document inference, and test both false approvals and false denials on exposed sentinel cases. Historical votes and the v123 stopping certificate must remain unchanged.

## Decision about another internal pool

**Do not freeze another pool yet.** Under the saved votes and no-retry rule, v123 could reach at most 49 complete pairs, scope 2 and measurement 4. This is a valid stopping proof for this audit run, but the reviewer errors mean it is not proof that the source distribution lacks suitable questions. Simply drawing another 100 with the same builder and rubric would repeat the structural and semantic failure modes. Nor should old invalid or disputed votes be rehabilitated by changing labels after seeing the deficit.

Before deciding a new one-time freeze, use only already exposed development material to (1) specify and test the multi-document audit contract on the cases above; (2) make reviewer responses mechanically reliable and preserve restricted raw invalid outputs; (3) revise query construction to target the missing relation rather than select an arbitrary visible sentence by annotated-answer retrieval; and (4) preflight complete paired states on the *proposed final shared corpus*, including token budget, before the one freeze. Keep fixed-state data quality separate from future model end-to-end accuracy. Once these checks pass, estimate the achievable pair and risk yield on an exposed pilot, freeze the candidate list and corpus once, then audit blind under the unchanged 50-pair and five-per-risk gates. The existing v123 pool stays exposed and cannot become a new unexposed acceptance set.
